📖 Social Story: Navigating Unexpected Changes

1. Sometimes, plans change suddenly: A class might be cancelled, or a meeting time might move. When this happens, it is normal to feel startled or uneasy.

2. It is okay to take a moment: I can take three slow breaths, drink water, or listen to a calming sound while my brain adjusts.

3. The change does not mean danger: I can check my updated schedule or ask: "What is the new plan?" People will understand that I need a moment to re-orient.

Body Sensation Mapping

• Fluttery stomach: Often means anxiety. Try sipping warm water.
• Clenched jaw / fists: Often means overstimulation. Try deep arm stretching.
• Heavy shoulders: Often means fatigue. Honor your need to rest.

🎓 Parent & Teacher Support Portal

Guidance, classroom accommodations, and collaborative advocacy tools to help neurodivergent learners thrive.

🏡 Supporting Your Child at Home

  • Externalize Routines: Hang visual picture checklists by the door, bathroom, and study desk.
  • Transition Warnings: Give 5-minute and 2-minute gentle warnings before switching activities.
  • De-Escalate, Don't Debate: When sensory overload strikes, prioritize quiet safety over immediate reasoning.
  • Praise Effort Over Outcome: Celebrate initiation, persistence, and self-advocacy rather than only grades.

🤝 School Advocacy & IEP/504 Meetings

Coming prepared to school meetings builds a collaborative partnership between families and teachers.

🏫 Sensory-Inclusive Classroom Strategies

  • Chunked Instructions: Give one step at a time; write instructions visibly on the board.
  • Alternative Formats: Permit audio books, speech-to-text, or mind-maps for assignments.
  • Sensory-Friendly Seating: Offer seating away from noisy air conditioners or high-traffic doors.
  • Fidget Friendly: Allow quiet tactile fidgets that assist regulation without distracting peers.

💡 Supporting Executive Function in Class

When a student misses an assignment, it is usually an executive dysfunction barrier rather than defiance or laziness.

"Can you show me your first step?" is far more productive than "Why haven't you started?"

📋 Evidence-Based School & Workplace Accommodations

Area Common Challenge Recommended Accommodation
ADHD Time blindness, task initiation Segmented due dates, visual timers, quiet test room with extra time
Dyslexia Reading fatigue, spelling anxiety Colored overlays, text-to-speech tools, spelling not penalized on content exams
Autism Sensory overload, sudden changes Advance warning of schedule shifts, quiet retreat pass, noise-cancelling headphones

🏥 Professional Resources & Specialist Guide

Learn about the different specialists who diagnose and support neurodevelopmental differences, and prepare for your appointments.

🧠

Clinical Psychologists

Conduct comprehensive neuropsychological assessments, evaluate cognitive profiles, executive functioning, and guide behavioral support plans.

⚕️

Neurologists

Medical doctors specializing in nervous system anatomy, brain health, neurological screenings, and medical management when appropriate.

🗣️

Speech-Language Pathologists

Support phonological processing for dyslexia, social pragmatic communication, AAC usage, and expressive language expansion.

🖐️

Occupational Therapists (OT)

Specialize in sensory integration, fine motor coordination, proprioception, and ergonomic adjustments for daily living.

📚

Special Educators

Implement customized multi-sensory teaching curriculums (e.g., Orton-Gillingham for dyslexia) and design IEP school accommodations.

🌐

Support Organizations

Global advocacy groups (such as CHADD, Understood.org, and the Autistic Self Advocacy Network) providing research and community resources.
